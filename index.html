About

Two languages, one habit of looking closely.

I'm a student at Seoul International School. I spend my time in two very different rooms — one where things have to compile, and one where paint has about ninety seconds before it dries the wrong way. They turn out to ask for the same thing: see what is actually in front of you before you commit to a mark.

The two rooms are less separate than they look. The drawing that won the Busan International Kids Art Fair in 2025 is a pair of scissors cutting through a paper city, and I called it 무한 루프를 끊어라 — Breaking the Infinite Loop. It sold off the wall.

The rest follows from there. I've competed in computer-science olympiads in London, Seoul and online; I've won that art fair three times; and I write — a research paper on social-media use and literacy, a documentary on Korean history, essays on plastic waste.

Most recently I spent four weeks inside the AI labs at Seoul National University, and I keep coming back to the same question: what can a fifteen-year-old actually build with these tools that someone else needs?
